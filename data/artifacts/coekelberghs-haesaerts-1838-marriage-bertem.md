> **N° 8.**
>
> Op heden **den Dertigsten October** duyzend acht honderd **acht en dertig**, ten **zeven**

## The forename question, which is what this act was opened for

[[haesaerts_anna_1810]] is written three ways across the acts that name her — *Anna* in 1838,
1853, 1873 and 1903, *Anna Maria* in two 1867 acts, *Anna Catharina* in an 1858 act. Her
own marriage act writes her **Anna**, plainly and three times over: in the large display
hand that opens her half of the act, and again in the operative sentence *dat door het
huwelyk vereenigd zyn Joannes Baptista Coekelberghs en Anna Haesaerts*. There is no
second forename, no abbreviation, and no space left where one was dropped.

That settles what she was called **at her wedding**. It does not by itself settle what she
was baptised, and it does not prove that the *Anna Maria* and *Anna Catharina* of the
later acts are the same woman rather than a relative — that remains a reading, and the
records stay unmerged on it. What the act does give is the test that would settle it: it
names her birth as **Berthem, 24 October 1810**, so the Bertem birth register for 1810
will say which forenames she was registered under. That film is not this one — this reel
carries Bertem births only from 1891.

## The trade question

The groom is **slagter** here, in a hand that leaves no doubt. That agrees with the 1853
and 1867 acts and disagrees with 1858, 1861 and 1873, where he is *landbouwer*. Nothing in
this act explains the move; it is recorded, not reconciled. His father and his
father-in-law are both *landbouwers*, and he himself is a *landbouwer* by 1873, so a
butcher in a farming village who ends up on the land is the unremarkable reading — but
this act cannot prove the two are one man, only that the 1838 groom was a butcher.

## What it does not settle

Nothing in this act names [[coekelberghs_maria_1848]], the daughter born ten years later, and nothing
in it gives a death. The chain from this couple to her still rests entirely on the 1873
Sint-Stevens-Woluwe and 1903 Kraainem acts naming her parents as Joannes Baptista
Coekelberghs and Anna Haesaerts of Bertem. Nor is the groom's own birth of 29 July 1809
confirmed from a birth register here — the act is reporting it, not proving it.
