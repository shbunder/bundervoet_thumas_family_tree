An estate division in which the parties are not Bundervoets but the **children of several
Bundervoet sisters**, each group standing in its late mother's place. The act is undated in the portion read — the register covers 1746-1755 and `date` records
only its opening year, not the act's own. Reassembled from deep-zoom tiles; **only the clause below has been read closely**, and the act runs longer
than the portion captured here.

> … van **Marie Joanne gheboren den 19 februarij 1712**, ende **Livinus de Smet gheboren
> den 30 maert 1719**, gewonnen bij wijlent **Passchijntien de Bundervoet**, van ghelijcken
> **suster van vollen bedde** was als vooren, volgens de wettelijcke brieven daer af
> uijtgegeven den 3 xbre 1735 …

So **Paschasia Bundervoet**, deceased by the date of the act, had by (per the machine
transcription of the same page) a **Pieter de Smet** two children with stated birth dates —
19 February 1712 and 30 March 1719 — and she is called a **full sister** of the other
parties.

The transcription layer puts two more sibling groups in the same act: three children of
**Frans de Moerloose** by the late **Joanna Bundervoet**, and four children of a van der
[Craissen / Waijlle — the two readings disagree] by another late Bundervoet sister.

**Nothing from this act has entered the tree.** The clause above is read from the image and
is sound; the rest rests on transcription that renders one surname two ways in two passes,
and the sisters' own father is nowhere in the portion read. What the act would give, read in
full, is a sibship of Bundervoet women with their married names and their children — which is
why it stays on the queue rather than being written up as settled.
