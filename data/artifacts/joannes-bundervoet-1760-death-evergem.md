The image the paulderidder tree attaches to Joannes Bundervoet (ca 1682–1760) as its
evidence for the death date, captioned there "Joannes Bundervoet Overlijdensregister
Evergem 1760".

**Not read, and therefore not treated as read.** The only version the site holds is
230 × 38 pixels — a single cropped line of the register, at a resolution where the
handwriting cannot be transcribed with any confidence. `original.JPG` and `large.JPG`
both return 404; this crop is all that was deposited. It is kept because it records
*what the tree's claim rests on*, which is worth knowing, and because the crop is
evidence that a register entry exists to be found.

So Joannes stays at confidence `sup`. Upgrading him needs the Evergem death register
for 1760 at a readable resolution — Rijksarchief `search.arch.be`, Evergem
overlijdens 1760 — where the entry could actually be read and the date, the age
("aged about 78") and any named survivors checked. That is the frontier this artifact
points at rather than closes.
