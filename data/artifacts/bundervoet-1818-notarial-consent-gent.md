Drawn by **Charles Le Begue, public notary residing at Gent**, on 21 October 1818, and
filed with the Rotterdam marriage a month later. The son, in Rotterdam, had instructed a
Rotterdam notary on 19 October to obtain his father's consent; Le Begue went to the
father's house in Gent, read the request to him and took his answer.

> Op heden een en twintigsten october een duysend acht honderd achthien hebbe ik
> onderschreven **Charles Le Begue** openbaere Notaris tot **Gend** residerende, provintie
> van Oostvlaenderen … ten verzoeke van Sieur **Livinus Cornelis Bundervoet, sone van
> Egidius by wylent Anne Marie Elisabeth Peters, Kuyper van beroep, woonende tot
> Rotterdam** provintie Zuyd Holland, my begeven ten persoone van de genoemde Sieur
> **Egidius Bundervoet, stekwercker, woonende binnen deese stad in de Ackerstraete numero
> twee en twintig, vader van den voornoemden mynen requirant** …
>
> [image 6] … tenderende aen den selven synen Vader te kennen te geeven syne Intentie van
> in egten huwelyke te treden met **Joffrauw Joanna Christina van Gerven oud acht en
> twintig jaeren, dogter van Hermanus en van Joanna Daalse** egtelieden, woonende by de
> voornoemde haere ouders binnen de stad Rotterdam …
>
> [image 8] … ter presentie van **Jacques Kerchove, cleermaeker**, ende **Jean Cropato,
> knopmaeker**, beede insetene deeser stad, getuygen hier over geroepen …
> Enregistré à Gand le vingt-deux Octobre 1818 … Vu par nous Président du Tribunal civil
> séant à Gand pour valoir légalisation de la signature ci-dessus de maître Ch. Le Begue
> Notaire en cette ville. Gand, le trente Octobre 1818.

What it fixes, beyond the baptism extract:

- **Egidius Bundervoet was alive in October 1818 and living at Ackerstraat 22, Gent**, with
  a trade: *stekwercker*. A street address in the city, thirty-one years after his marriage.
- **Anna Maria Elisabeth Peeters was already dead** — *by wylent*, "by the late" — so she
  died between her son's birth in 1788 and October 1818. That is the window for her death
  act, and it is also what makes room for Egidius's second marriage, to Maria Francisca
  Ledent.
- **The son's own trade: kuiper**, a cooper, at Rotterdam.
- The father's forename is given as *Egidius* by a notary who stood in the room with him,
  which is the strongest available answer to the *Petrus* of the Rotterdam civil records.

The witnesses are Gent tradesmen — a tailor and a button-maker — not kin, so they add
nothing to the family; they are recorded here because the act names them and the note is
where that belongs rather than in a person record.
