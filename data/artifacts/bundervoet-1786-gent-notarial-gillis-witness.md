The closing lines of a notarial act, reassembled from deep-zoom tiles. The rest of the
page is blank — the act body is on the preceding image.

> en presence de S.r **M. Batkin** et **G: Bundervoet** temoins a ce requis ce
> **19 aout 1786**
>
> *signed:* Jacques Fruot · M. Batkin · **gillis bundervoet** · A. Vergult, nots pub

**What it is, and what it is not.** He appears as a **witness** — *temoin* — not as a party.
The act says nothing about his family, his age or his trade, and the deed it closes is
somebody else's business.

**Its whole value is the autograph.** He signs **gillis bundervoet** in a fluent, practised
hand, and the same signature in the same form closes his son's baptism entry in
[[bundervoet-1788-baptism-extract-gent-sintmaarten]] two years later. That is a second
attestation that the Gent Egidius wrote his name in the Flemish form, and that he was in the
city in August 1786 — fifteen months before his marriage.

**A discrepancy it sharpens rather than settles.** [[bundervoet_livinus_gent]]'s contemporary
namesake, the Livinus Bundervoet who stood godfather in 1788, *declared he could not write*.
This Gillis clearly could. The two are different men on that evidence alone, which is worth
noting because a single Gent Bundervoet household would not usually split that way.
