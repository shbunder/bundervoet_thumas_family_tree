**What this is, precisely.** A finding aid, not a record index. It says the surname
occurs in that parish's registers and which of the three registers hold it; it does not
give a person, a date or a parent. So nothing here can be grafted. What it does is tell
you which registers to read, which for the pre-1796 era — where 158 of this tree's people
sit and no civil act exists — is the question that has been unanswerable until now.

**Why it matters for objective 3.** "Connect all Bundervoets" is a forest problem, and
this is the first map of the forest: sixteen parishes around Gent held Bundervoets in the
parish-register era, and this tree descends from the Evergem group alone. Vinderhoute,
Wondelgem and Zomergem carry all three registers, which makes them the places a
neighbouring family would be reconstructable from.

**And for the line already here.** Evergem shows `dho`, so its burial register covers
[[bundervoet_joannes_1682]]'s death of 7 November 1760 and [[bundervoet_christoffel_1709]]'s of 1786 — the first of
which is the act whose only surviving scan is an illegible 230×38 crop.
