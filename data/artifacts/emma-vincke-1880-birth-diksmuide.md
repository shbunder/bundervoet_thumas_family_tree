**This is the index analysis, not the act image.** The Rijksarchief's volunteers
transcribed the act; the scan itself is referenced only as *SCAN 392 GSU* and is not
linked from this page. So this is `sup` evidence, however authoritative the archive.

What the page states, in full:

> Gegevens van de akte **nr. 14** · Aktetype **Geboorteakten** · Project *Burgerlijke
> stand - Geboorteakten - Provincie West-Vlaanderen* · Plaats **Diksmuide** ·
> Beschrijving Geboorteakte · Opmerking **SCAN 392 GSU** · Aktedatum **24/01/1880**
>
> **Kind** — Emma Celesta Vincke · Geboorteplaats **Diksmuide** · Geboortedatum **23/01/1880**
> **Vader** — Lucien Julianus Vincke
> **Moeder** — Ludovica Maria Vanalderweireldt

The act date is one day after the birth date, which is the ordinary gap between a birth
and its declaration — the same offset noted at §35 of the research log for AGATHA
generally, and it is why the act date must never be read as the birth date.

**Why this is the act detail page and not the search modal.** An earlier attempt captured
the result-list modal instead, and that image was discarded: the modal is scrollable, so
the *Vader* and *Moeder* rows visible at its foot belonged to the results table behind it —
a different act (08/10/1873, Romanie Elodie Vincke). The act's own page, reached by direct
id, shows one act and nothing else, and it renders without a session. Capture the act, not
the search result about it.

Independently of the memorial card that [[vincke_emma_1880]] already rested on, this puts her
birth date, her birthplace and both parents on civil registration.
