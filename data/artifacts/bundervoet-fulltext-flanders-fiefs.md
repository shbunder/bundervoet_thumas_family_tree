The answer to the problem that has blocked half this tree.

**159 hits** for the surname, in manuscripts nobody has ever name-indexed, read by machine
transcription of the page images themselves. The collections are not registers at all —
feudal court records, notarial deeds, militia lists — which is why no index reaches them.

From *Flanders. Nobility Records* alone, with the years the collection spans listed as
**1463, 1477, 1493, 1510, 1587, 1604, 1650, 1652, 1653, 1655, 1674, 1675, 1687, 1700, 1701,
1774, 1792**:

> **Catherine Bundervoet**, veuve de Jean De Smet — Meirelbeke, 1 April **1587**
> **Lievin Bundervoet, fils de François**, tient un fief — Alost, 3 March **1687**
> **Lievin Bundervoet, fils de Pasquier**, tient un fief — 16 February **1700**
> **Pasquier Bundervoet, fils de Lievin**, tient un fief — 16 February **1700**

Those last three are a **three-generation chain stated outright** — François → Lievin →
Pasquier → Lievin — in exactly the century where this tree's Bundervoet line
([[petrus_bundervoet1560]] ~1560, [[livinus_bundervoet]] ~1600, [[segerius_bundervoet]]
~1615) rests on member trees alone.

Also present: *Gent, East Flanders. Notarial Records 1746-1755* and *1753-1757*, with
Bundervoet family transactions naming wives and siblings, and a Belgian militia record
giving a mother as "Bundervoet Petronilla Catherine, née à Gand".

**Nothing here is grafted, and nothing here should be.** These are not our people until an
identity is established, and a fief-holder called Lievin in Alost is not [[livinus_bundervoet]]
of Evergem because they share a name and a century. What this artifact records is that the
*source* exists and is reachable — which for the 158 people in this tree born before civil
registration was the missing thing, not the willingness to look.

**Why it matters more than another index.** Every venue in this registry searches
transcriptions someone else made. This searches the manuscript. It is the only route to a
person who never appears in a parish register — and feudal and notarial records are where
property, and therefore descent, was actually written down.
