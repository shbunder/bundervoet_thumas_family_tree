Married [[ludovica_bossin]] and, through her, is [[bossin]]'s brother-in-law. This
record previously held nothing beyond that marriage and the bride's parents, from S24's
bare index row. It is filled in (2026-07-27) from the civil marriage act itself.

**Own marriage act, Kraainem, 25 February 1884** — a fuller record of the same wedding
S24 already carried, not a replacement of it (see the date/place note below). It states
his birth directly: **Alsemberg, 11 May 1858**, and his trade, *schrijnwerker*. His
parents are named: **Egidius Swaelens**, hovenier of Braine-l'Alleud, and **Joanna
Ackermans**, already dead — Alsemberg, 27 December 1877 — see [[egidius_swaelens]] and
[[joanna_ackermans]].

**Corroborated internally**: standing witness at [[henricus_bossin]]'s wedding — Kraainem,
23 November 1887 — he is given as 29, *schrijnwerker*, resident Kraainem by then. Age 29
on that date is an exact arithmetic match to the 1858-05-11 birth this act gives, and
shows he had relocated to his wife's commune within three years of marrying.

**Death dated (2026-07-27)**: named as [[ludovica_bossin]]'s deceased "vorige partner
bruid" in her 1901 remarriage act — Kraainem, 30 May 1901 — which gives his death as
**Kraainem, 7 October 1898**. Two identifiers make this unambiguous rather than a
namesake's death: the rare triple-forename-plus-surname combination, and the bride named
alongside him in that same act is independently confirmed as this tree's
[[ludovica_bossin]] by an exact match on her own birth date and place.

**A DISCREPANCY, left open rather than overwritten.** S24's declaration gives the
marriage as **17 February 1884, Alsemberg**; this civil act gives **25 February 1884,
Kraainem** — eight days and one commune apart. Banns published at Alsemberg (her side, or
his birthplace) followed by the act itself at Kraainem (her residence) is plausible, but
that is inference, not proof, and nothing found this pass settles it. Per the standing
practice on this exact kind of conflict (see [[guilielmus_bossin]] and
[[egidius_peremans]]'s one-year death dispute, research-log §69), the frontmatter
`married`/`place` fields stay at the earlier-held S24 values; the civil act's date and
place are recorded here, in prose, as the fuller account of the event rather than a
silent replacement.

**NOT PROVEN — a frontier, not a link.** A second witness at his own 1884 wedding,
**Franciscus Swaelens**, 24, resident **Sint-Genesius-Rode**, is also a *schrijnwerker* —
the groom's own trade. No act states a relationship, and he appears exactly once in the
held corpus. Same surname, same trade and a plausible age together read as one
correlated signal, not two independent ones: a trade runs in families and apprenticeships,
so it does not count alongside the surname as a second identifier. Settling this needs
either the register image or an act naming Franciscus's own parents.
