**The 17-vs-25 February discrepancy is SETTLED, not merely reconciled by inference.**
Both documents now say it outright, not just plausibly. The Kraainem marriage act states,
in its own words, that the banns were published "op de Zondagen tienden en zeventienden
Februari laatstleden" — the tenth and seventeenth of February — at Kraainem and Alsemberg.
Independently, the **Alsemberg banns register itself** was read: acts nrs. 7 and 8, each
printed **AKTE VAN HUWELIJKSAFKONDIGING** (a banns publication, not a marriage), dated
Sunday 10 February ("voor de eerste maal afgekondigd") and Sunday 17 February ("voor de
tweede maal afgekondigd") 1884. **S24's "17 February 1884, Alsemberg" was never a wrong
date — it was the second banns publication, correctly read off the FamilySearch index and
then misread here as a marriage date.** The marriage itself is **25 February 1884,
Kraainem**, now the value this record and [[ludovica_bossin]]'s carry, in agreement as the
data model requires; the two banns dates are recorded here, in prose, rather than
discarded, since they are a real and dated event, just not this one.

**Franciscus Swaelens is the groom's brother — the act states it, closing a refusal this
tree made on the strength of inference alone.** The witness clause reads: *"Franciscus
Swaelens, schrijnwerker, oud vier en twintig jaren, wonende te Sint-Genesius Rhode,
**broeder des bruidegoms**"* — brother of the groom, in the register's own words, not
inferred from the shared surname and shared trade this record had previously (rightly)
refused to treat as two identifiers. He is now entered as [[franciscus_swaelens]], son of
[[egidius_swaelens]] and [[joanna_ackermans]]. **The methodological point stands
regardless of the outcome**: the earlier refusal was the correct call on the evidence
then in hand — a trade running in a family is one correlated signal, not two independent
ones — and it was overturned not by a better score but by reading the document itself.
That is the loop working as designed, not a correction of an error.
