Roland's only sister, born in the early-to-mid 1940s — in England if she was born
alongside him during the family's wartime years there, in Oostende if before or after.
Her birth record is one of the two routes that would confirm Gustaaf × Simonne as a
couple, the other being Roland's own 1943 certificate.

MERGED (July 2026). This record and a second one, `roland_sis`, described the same
person: both were daughters of Gustaaf × Simonne, both were "Roland's sister", both
born in the 1940s. `roland_sis` was written when the family could recall a sister but
not her name, and this one after the name came out; nothing ever distinguished them.
The family knows of one sister, and Gustaaf's record likewise says one daughter. The
two are now one, with `roland_sis` deleted rather than left as a second copy.

NAME. "Rita" is family recollection, held with some confidence but not documented.

SURNAME UNCERTAIN — and deliberately so. Her brother's 1943 Tottenham registration is
known to have mangled the Flemish name, and the family does not know how Rita's own
was written down. The spelling here is the family's form, not a transcription of any
record: treat "De Keyser" as a placeholder until a document settles it. Variants
already seen or plausible for an English registrar: Dekeyser, Dekeyster, De Kayser,
Keyser. Any index search must run across all of them rather than the Flemish form
alone — the wrong spelling is the likeliest reason such a search comes back empty.

FRONTIER: England & Wales GRO birth index, Edmonton registration district (which
covered Tottenham) and its neighbours, 1940–1949, mother's maiden name Vandewalle.
The mother's-maiden-name field is the strong discriminator — it survives whatever the
registrar did to the surname, and it would tie Simonne to this family at the same time.
