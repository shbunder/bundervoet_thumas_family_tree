**A REFUSAL ANSWERED BY THE DOCUMENT (2026-07-27).** He stands witness at his brother
[[swaelens]]'s wedding — Kraainem, 25 February 1884 — aged 24, resident
**Sint-Genesius-Rode**, a *schrijnwerker*, the groom's own trade. Research-log §72 found
him in the same act and explicitly refused to graft him as a relative: a shared surname
plus a shared trade is one correlated signal, not the two independent identifiers Rule 1
requires, since a trade runs in families and apprenticeships. **The register image, read
this pass, states the relationship outright and removes the need to infer it**: *"Franciscus
Swaelens, schrijnwerker, oud vier en twintig jaren, wonende te Sint-Genesius Rhode,
**broeder des bruidegoms**"* — brother of the groom, in the act's own words. He also
signs the register himself ("Fs Swaelens").

That makes him a son of [[egidius_swaelens]] and [[joanna_ackermans]], alongside
[[swaelens]]. No birth date is stated on the act — only his age, 24, on 25 February 1884 —
so this record holds the year span that age permits, **1859..1860**, and no point year.

**The methodological point, not just the outcome.** §72's refusal was the right call on
the evidence then in hand: no act named a relationship, and the surname-plus-trade
correlation is exactly the kind of single, non-independent signal Rule 1 exists to keep
out of the tree. It was overturned by reading the primary document itself, not by a better
score or a change of mind — the loop working as designed, in both directions.
