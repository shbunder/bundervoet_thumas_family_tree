Named as the child in a civil birth act at **Bertem, 1858-01-15**, which names his father
as *Joannes Baptista Coekelberghs* — [[jb_coekelberghs]], who declared the birth in person,
aged 48, born at Bertem — and so places this person in the tree by the act rather than by a
name.

The act is held in the harvested corpus and reached through Open Archives:
https://www.openarchieven.nl/abl:a0b8c9a0-322e-f254-e5f4-efcd42d1f8e0

`sup`: an indexed act, not the register image. **No `sex` is recorded**, because the harvested
mention carries only the relation role and never a sex, and this project does not read a sex
off a forename.

RETRACTED IN PART (2026-07-26): **the mother link has been removed.** This record was
written with `mother: anna_haesaerts`, but the act does not name her. It names
*Anna **Catharina** Haesaerts* — precisely the forename variant that §51 and §53
deliberately left **unmerged** with the plain *Anna Haesaerts* of the 1838, 1853, 1873 and
1903 acts. Grafting this child to her would have silently resolved an open question by
side effect, which is the way a tree acquires a decision nobody made.

The father side is not in doubt: he declares the birth himself, his age and birthplace
agree with the 1838 marriage act read in §53, and Bertem is the commune. So the link
upward stands and the link across does not.

FRONTIER — one document settles it either way: the **1858 Bertem register image**, or
[[anna_haesaerts]]'s own death act, Bertem 8 January 1880. Until one is read, this record
has a father and no mother, and that is the honest shape of what is known.
