Younger sister of [[bossin]], and one of four known children of [[guilielmus_bossin]] and
[[peremans]] — a fourth, [[joannes_bossin51]] (b. 1851), was found in the same pass that
day-dated this birth.

**Birth dated to the day (2026-07-27), superseding the declaration-date reading.** A
separate civil birth act — Sint-Stevens-Woluwe nr. 18 (S33) — names her directly as the
child, states an "hour of birth" of 8:00, and gives its own registration date
(*SourceDate*) as the same day, 5 October 1853. Across this corpus, a Geboorte act's
EventDate and its SourceDate ordinarily differ by one or two days — a child registered a
day or two after the birth — which is what made the earlier FamilySearch declaration date
(S24) read as "probably a day late," on the same reasoning already applied to her sister
[[bossin]]'s birth. Here, uniquely, the two dates coincide: an hour is stated, the role is
*Kind* rather than a declarant, and there is no registration lag to allow for. That
internal evidence — same-day EventDate and SourceDate, plus a stated hour of birth — is
why this act is read as giving the birth itself rather than a declaration a day behind it,
and the field now carries **1853-10-05** as a day-level date rather than a year with a
`raw` declaration note.

She married **Joannes Baptista Julianus Swaelens** at **Alsemberg on 17 February 1884**, at
about thirty. The marriage index names her parents as *Guilielmus Bossin* and *Joanna Maria
Peremans* — a fourth spelling of her mother's forename, after the *Joanna Catharina Jacoba* of
her mother's own act, the *Anna Catharina* of her sister's 1872 act, and the *Anna Catharina*
of these birth declarations. One woman, four clerks.

That variation is why the marriage is attached at `sup` and not more. The birth row and the
marriage row agree on the surname pair, the father's forename and a consistent age, which is
enough to record — but the register images would settle whether the 1853 child and the 1884
bride are the same Ludovica.
