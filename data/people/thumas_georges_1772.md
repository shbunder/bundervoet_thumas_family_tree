CHECKED AND REJECTED (July 2026) — the corpus's best match for this record is not him.
It offers *Lambert Thumas*, who died at **Brussels on 28 February 1801**, and scores it 46
bits on surname, place of origin (Grez-Doiceau), a mother and a spouse. Ours died at
Grez-Doiceau in **1863**, sixty-two years later.

What produced the score is worth naming, because it will recur: the agreement on "mother"
and "spouse" is on FORENAMES only. That man's mother was Marie Joseph **Docq** and his wife
Marie Catherine **Becquevort**; ours are Marie Catherine **Noël** and Marie Anne Catherine
**Quinart**. Two women called Marie Catherine in one Walloon village is no evidence at all,
and the surnames — the part that would have been evidence — disagree outright.

A high score with a dead man's date sixty years out is the shape of a coincidence, not a
find. Left ungrafted and uncited.
