**The best-placed candidate for the generation above the Gent patriarchs, and deliberately
not linked to anyone.** He appears twice, a year apart, in the two acts read for
[[bundervoet_egidius]]'s family:

- **Witness at Egidius's marriage**, Gent Sint-Michiels-Noord, 17 April 1787 — *testibus
  Livino Bundervoet nato in B: Mariae habitante in hac parochia*. Born in the same
  Onze-Lieve-Vrouw parish as the groom, and living in the same parish as the couple. He
  **signed the register**, in a confident hand, *Lieven bundervoet*.
- **Godfather at Egidius's first child's baptism**, Sint-Maarten, Gent, 22 April 1788 —
  *Susceperunt Livinus Bundervoet qui dixit se non posse scribere*.

Witness at the wedding and godfather to the first child, out of the groom's own birth
parish: in a Flemish parish register that is what a brother or a father looks like.
**Nothing here is grafted.** Neither act states a relationship to Egidius, and a shared
surname plus a shared parish is one identifier where the rules want two — so this record
carries no `father`, no `siblings` and no link of any kind.

**A discrepancy kept rather than smoothed.** In 1787 he signs his own name; in 1788 the
clerk records that he *declared he could not write* and a mark was made for him. Both are
first-hand entries a year apart in the same city. Either the 1788 note attaches to the
wrong person, or these are two different Livinus Bundervoets — and the Gent Sint-Pieters
register does hold several. This is exactly the kind of near-miss the two-identifier rule
exists for, and it is why the two attestations above are held on one record only
provisionally.

**What would settle him:** [[bundervoet_egidius]]'s baptism in Onze-Lieve-Vrouw /
Sint-Pieters, Gent, which names Egidius's parents. If a Livinus is among them, this record
is his father; if a Livinus is a brother baptised in the same register, it is his brother.
