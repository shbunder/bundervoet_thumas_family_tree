STRONG LEAD, NOT GRAFTED (July 2026). A Brugge marriage act of 16 August 1851 ([[S12]])
records a **Franciscus Léopoldus Smessaert** marrying an **Anna Francisca Morbeé**, and
names both sets of parents: Paulus Smessaert x Isabella Claeys for him, Petrus Josephus
Morbeé x Joanna De Clerck for her.

Three things fit: the groom's triple name is distinctive and matches exactly, the bride's
forenames match exactly, and a marriage in 1851 sits right for a son born at Oostende in
1857. Brugge and Oostende are neighbours.

One thing does not: the bride is **Morbeé**, and this tree calls her **Morree**. That is a
disagreement, not a silence, and it is the only surname either of these two records has.
Everything else here — both of them have no dates, no places and no source of their own —
comes from the wernero tree, so there is nothing independent to break the tie with. Under
the two-identifier rule this is a lead, and it stays one: no parents have been grafted.

DECISIVE RECORD: [[joannesf_s]]'s Oostende birth act of 12 January 1857, which would give
his mother's surname as the registrar wrote it. If it says Morbeé, this act is ours and
four new ancestors come with it; if it says Morree, the couple above are somebody else's.
