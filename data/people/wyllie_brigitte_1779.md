Bore the Scottish-origin surname Wyllie in West-Flanders; widowed young in 1808, remarried
in 1818 (below).

BIRTH DATE OPEN, NOT RESOLVED EITHER WAY (2026-07-27). This record held 22 February 1779.
Two independent acts — her own 1803 marriage
(`data/artifacts/vanalderweireldt-wyllie-1803-marriage-diksmuide.md`) and her 1818
remarriage (`data/artifacts/looten-wyllie-1818-marriage-diksmuide.md`) — both instead
transcribe her birth as 22 October 1779, the day and month transposed against what was held.
Neither reading satisfies both acts' own stated ages, checked by computation: born
22/10/1779 gives age 23 at the 1803 wedding (the act says 24) but age 38 at the 1818 one
(the act says 38, correct); born 22/02/1779 gives age 24 in 1803 (correct) but age 39 in
1818 (the act says 38, wrong). Neither candidate is dropped in favour of the other — both
are recorded here as competing — so the point date is reduced to the year both
transcriptions and the arithmetic agree on. An index-level self-reported age is not primary
evidence either way, so even resolving this conflict would not reach `doc`; only her own
birth or baptismal record would.

REMARRIED, FOUND WITHOUT LOOKING FOR IT (2026-07-27). Diksmuide, 25 June 1818, act nr. 82,
RAB ID 85255, microfilm 1166237 (`data/artifacts/looten-wyllie-1818-marriage-diksmuide.md`)
— she married [[looten_francois_1785]], a widow at 38. **What identifies this bride as this
record is the act's own Previous-Partners table**, not the name agreeing alone: VANALDERWEIRELDT
| Antoine | Diksmuide | 06/07/1808 — naming her first husband, his place and a third value
for his death day. Her parents are given identically to the 1803 act. The methodological
point worth keeping: a Vanalderweireldt-only search would never have found this marriage,
because by 1818 she carries a different surname — it surfaced only because her own 1824
death act still names Looten as her partner, and the search worked back from there. See
[[looten_francois_1785]] for the marriage and the children.

DEATH DATE CONFIRMED, NOT RETRACTED — A DELIBERATE CONTRAST (2026-07-27). Her own
death-index row (Diksmuide act nr. 198, RAB ID 2298609, Certificate Date 10/11/1824, Date
column blank) has exactly the same blank-Date-column shape that cost [[vanalderweireldt_antoine_1780]] his
day-level precision. Here it does not cost this record anything: both her sons'
own 1828 marriage acts — [[vanalderweireldt_carolus_1808]]'s and [[vanalderweireldt_petrus_1803]]'s — independently state
her decease as 8 November 1824, this record's held value, a two-day lag behind the
certificate date rather than a contradiction of it. Kept at day precision because two later,
independent civil acts agree — where Antoine's three sources did not agree with each other
at all.

FOUR MORE NAMES, NONE LINKED. Her own parents, as both acts name them: **Wyllie François
Antoine × Deman** [forename open]. The father leans toward acceptance — named identically
in the 1803 and 1818 acts, and corroborated by an exact death date and place (30 September
1811, Diksmuide) given on the 1818 act. Her mother's forename is not settled: the 1803 act
writes "Deman Brigitte", the 1818 act "Deman Catharine", and her own death-index row renders
her fully as "Deman Catherine Bregite" — read together, this looks like a compound name
split differently by different clerks (the same pattern runs through this whole cluster:
Isabella Claire/Clara, this record's own Josephine Joanna, Antoine's Joseph Albert), not a
contradiction. No single forename is asserted, and neither parent is linked, per the same
standard applied on [[vanalderweireldt_antoine_1780]]'s record.
