CORROBORATED (July 2026) as a mother, by her daughter's Kraainem marriage act of
20 June 1872, which names the bride's parents as Guilielmus Bossin and *Anna* Catharina
Peremans. The act's forenames are shorter than the ones this record carries — Anna
Catharina against Joanna Catharina Jacoba — which is ordinary for a civil act quoting a
parent, but it is a difference, and it is recorded rather than smoothed over.

ACT READ (July 2026): named in her daughter's 1872 marriage act as *huishoudster*, living
at Kraainem. The act calls her Anna Catharina; this record calls her Joanna Catharina
Jacoba. Both stand.

ACT READ (2026-07-26): her own marriage act — Zaventem nr. 1, 24 February 1846 (S19),
read as an image. It **settles the forename dispute above in this record's favour**: the
act that married her calls her *Joanna Catharina Jacoba Peremans*, in full, which is what
this tree held. The shorter *Anna Catharina* of her daughter's 1872 act was the act
quoting a parent from memory, and it is now the outlier rather than a rival reading.

She was **born at Zaventem** and was **21**, which confirms the 1824 from a civil act and
matches her husband year for year. Her occupation at marriage was *arbeidster*.

The act names her parents [[egidius_peremans]] and [[joanna_verelst]], both already dead
— he at Zaventem on 6 March 1837, she at Zaventem on 19 December 1843 — so she married at
twenty-one an orphan on both sides. And it records something registers rarely bother with:
she declared, with her witnesses, that **she had never known her grandparents nor where
they died**. That is a documented dead end, not a gap to keep searching by name.
