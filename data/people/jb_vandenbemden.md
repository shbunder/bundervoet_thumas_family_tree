Father of the groom in his son [[hendrik_vdb]]'s marriage act — Sint-Stevens-Woluwe nr. 6,
24 April 1873.

**What the act does not say about him is the informative part.** His wife
[[elisabeth_langes]] is given a residence, a trade and the words *present and consenting*;
he is given a name and nothing else — no commune, no occupation, no consent. That is the
register's usual shape for a parent already dead, and it is why this record carries no
death entry at all rather than a `raw` note claiming he was alive: a bare name is not a
statement either way, and inventing "alive on 24 April 1873" from silence would be exactly
the guess the grammar has no syntax for.

He confirms — and corrects — an unverified Geneanet claim this tree had been carrying on
his son's record, which named him *Joannes Baptista Vandenbemden*. The act's own spelling
is *Jan Baptist Van den Bemden*, and it is followed here.

`sup`: an AGATHA act analysis, not the register image.

FRONTIER — his own marriage to Elisabeth Agatha Langes, which the Everberg or Kortenberg
registers should hold for roughly 1840–1849 and which would name his parents; and his
death, which on the reading above falls before April 1873. Two Van den Bemden witnesses
stood at his son's wedding — *Lodewijk*, 40, daglooner of Sint-Stevens-Woluwe, and
*Willem*, 26, landbouwer of Schaarbeek — and one of them is likely his son or his brother.
Neither is grafted: the act states no kinship for either.
