An elder sister of [[marie_vanbergen]], and the first of that generation this tree has
besides her. Her marriage act — **Hamme, 18 May 1882**, read as the register image — names
her as *meerderjarige dochter van* **Joannes Franciscus Van Den Berghe**, *metser*, sixty-five
years old, living at Hamme, present at the wedding and consenting, *en van wijlen* **Ida
Vermandel, overleden te Hamme den eenen twintigste Juny achttien honderd vyf en zestig**.

**What carries the graft is that death date.** Twenty-one June 1865 at Hamme is exactly what
[[ida_vermandel]]'s own death act says — a different act, a different clerk, seventeen years
apart, and neither knowing about the other. The second identifier is the father's trade,
*metser*, which is the same trade [[jan_van_bergen]] is given in [[marie_vanbergen]]'s 1888
Oostende death act. Her forename and her mother's surname are not doing the work here.

**It also settles a spelling that was blocking three grafts.** The register writes this
family **Van Bergen** in 1865 — Ida's death act, and the 1865 alphabetical *tafel*, which
files it among Van-acker, Van-Bergen, Van-Bochstael, Van-Bogaert, so it is not a *Van den*
name there. The 1882 index writes **Van Den Berghe**. That difference was the reason three
possible siblings sat unrecorded, because a clerk's drift and two families sharing an
unusually rare wife's surname look identical from an index. Reading the act resolved it as
one family. The surname here follows the act that was actually read.

The act records her as a widow before this marriage. Her first husband's forename is not
confidently legible on the image and no name is recorded from it — a name read at the edge
of legibility is a guess, and this project has no field for one.

`doc`: the register image was read, by stitching the deep-zoom tiles on the
`sg30p0.familysearch.org` origin. Her birth year comes from her stated age and is not itself
proved by an act.

FRONTIER — her own Hamme birth act of about 1849, which would give her birth to the day;
and her first marriage, which the Hamme registers should hold before May 1882.
