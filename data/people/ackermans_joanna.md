Named as mother of the groom, already dead, in her son [[swaelens_joannes_1858]]'s marriage act —
Kraainem, 25 February 1884 — which gives her death as **Alsemberg, 27 December 1877**.
That is a second, checkable fact alongside the bare parent-name pair, and it is what lifts
this parentage above a name coincidence: a dead mother's exact place and date, six years
before the wedding, is not the kind of detail an act invents.

**UPGRADED TO `doc` (2026-07-27) — no longer single-source.** The Kraainem act's own
register image was read, confirming this exactly. Independently, and before that act
existed, the **Alsemberg marriage banns** (nrs. 7 and 8, 10 and 17 February 1884) — a
different commune, register series and clerk — name the same pair, **Egidius Swaelens x
Joanna Ackermans, overleden**. Her identity is not corroborated a second time as to the
death date itself (the banns omit it), but the parent pairing is: two unconnected
registers, not one act alone.
