# Objective 3 — the Gent Bundervoets: the venue was the wall, and one of the six is now attached

Pass run 2026-07-27 in a parallel session while another run held the hot files. **Nothing
below has been grafted, labelled or logged.** Evidence in the three sibling `.json` files.
Read `README.md` in this directory first.

> **Revision note.** An earlier draft of this file argued that the surname radiates from
> Evergem and that Gent is a satellite. **That is retracted** — see §2. Geneanet's archive
> index holds ~100 Bundervoet marriages in Gent city parishes from **1602** onward,
> continuous to 1796, which is deeper and denser than anything documented on the Evergem
> line. The earlier claim was an artifact of which venues had been harvested.

## The result

§73 closed the Gent cluster as "six distinct families, none connects." That was true of
**Open Archives Belgium**, the only venue consulted, which is genuinely exhausted at 396
records. Three other indexes were never opened. Between them they hold **the marriage acts
of four of the six patriarchs**, and one of those four now links — through his wife's name
— to a Bundervoet who emigrated to Rotterdam and whose birth date is known to the day.

## 2. RETRACTED — "the surname radiates from Evergem"

The earlier draft measured every stated Bundervoet birthplace in the held Belgian corpus
(n=118: Evergem 47, Gent 29, Oostende 17, Wachtebeke 9, Kluizen 5 …) and concluded Evergem
was the origin. **Withdrawn.** That corpus is Open Archives, which for this region is
memorial cards plus 19th–20th-century civil registration — Evergem-heavy by construction.
The Gent city **parish** registers, 1600–1796, were in none of the harvested venues.

Geneanet's archive index holds Bundervoet marriages at **Gent Sint-Martinus from 1602,
1605, 1610, 1625, 1628–31, 1634–36, 1639, 1641–42, 1645, 1648–50, 1656 …** and at **Gent
Sint-Pieters continuously through the 18th century to 1796** — with deaths at Sint-Pieters
from 1653. Two hundred years of continuous presence, **older than any documented record on
our Evergem line**, whose evidence begins ~1615.

By 1682 the Gent group was large enough to marry within itself: **Livinus Bundervoet ×
Martina Bundervoet, Gent Sint-Martinus, 1682.**

What survives from the earlier argument is only the weak form: the surname is rare (391 of
4,276,988 corpus mentions, **13.4 bits**, no spelling variants) and every Belgian
occurrence falls within ~20 km. Which of Gent or Evergem is upstream is **open**, and on
present evidence Gent has the deeper record.

## 3. THE LINK — Egidius Bundervoet × Anna Maria Elisabeth Peters

Two records, from two venues neither of which the project had opened:

> **Marriage, Gent (Sint-Michiels Noord), 1787.**
> **BUNDERVOET Egidius × PETERS Anna Maria Elisabeth.**
> Geneanet archive index
>
> **Marriage, Rotterdam, 25 Nov 1818** and **death, Rotterdam, 28 Feb 1872.**
> **Livinus Cornelis Bundervoet**, age 28 / "83 years and 10 months", **born Gent**;
> father **Petrus Bundervoet**, mother **Anna Maria Elizabeth Peeters**.
> Four Rotterdam population-register entries give **born 22 May 1788, Gent**.
> Stadsarchief Rotterdam 999-06 reg. 1818B f. b66v; 999-09 reg. 1872A f. a193v act 1872.741

**The mother's name matches on all four tokens** — *Anna Maria Elisabeth Peters* /
*Anna Maria Elizabeth Peeters* — in the same city, with a marriage in **1787** and a first
child born **22 May 1788**. Three independent identifiers: the full given-name string of a
woman (not a common single forename), the place, and a chronology that fits to the month.

**And Egidius Bundervoet is patriarch #6 of §73's six** — the "Egidius Bundervoet × Maria
Francisca Ledent" whose wife was born 1777 at Lokeren and died 1853 at Gent. She cannot
have been his wife in 1787 (she was ten). So **Peters is the first marriage and Ledent the
second**, and Livinus Cornelis is Egidius's son by the first.

If that holds, it is the first time any of the six has been connected to anything beyond
its own death-index cluster.

**The conflict, stated rather than smoothed over.** Both Rotterdam records give the
father's forename as **Petrus**, the Gent marriage gives **Egidius**. Under the project's
rules a stated conflict vetoes, so **this is NOT GRAFTABLE as it stands**. The two readings
are (a) one man, with the Rotterdam clerk recording the wrong forename or a double name
*Egidius Petrus* — the son emigrated and may have reported it from memory; or (b) two
different Bundervoet men who each married a woman called Anna Maria Elisabeth Peters in
Gent, which is close to impossible for a 13.4-bit surname. **(a) is far more likely, and
that is still not a document.**

**Resolve it by reading two acts**, both now precisely addressable:
1. the **Gent Sint-Michiels Noord marriage of 1787** — it names the groom in full and his
   parents, which reaches the generation above patriarch #6;
2. the **Gent baptism of 22 May 1788** — it names the father as the parish clerk had him,
   and its **godparents, who in a Flemish baptism register are almost always kin**.

Cheapest route to (2) first: a Dutch civil marriage required the groom's birth certificate,
so for a foreign-born groom **the Gent extract was filed with the Rotterdam 1818
huwelijksbijlagen**, which Stadsarchief Rotterdam has scanned. That gets the Gent act
without opening a Gent register.

**A hypothesis worth one act, not a claim.** *Petrus × Maria Wille* marries in 1787 and has
a son in 1790; *Petrus × Isabella De Coster* marries in 1790 and has a daughter in 1791. If
Maria Wille died at or soon after the 1790 birth, these are **one man remarrying**, and two
of §73's six collapse into one family. Both acts are at Sint-Pieters and would say.

- **Maria Anna Bundervoet × Livinus De Smet — married 1781, Gent Sint-Pieters.** Their

A tight intermarrying Gent network, not six unrelated households.

## 5. Two separate Flanders→Rotterdam migrations, not one

The Dutch material is **two unrelated populations**, and they must not be merged.

**(a) 1631–1727, from Bergen op Zoom.** Three generations around **Michiel Geertssen
Bundervoet** (Bergen op Zoom notarial acts 1631 and 1655; Rotterdam from 1648). His
children carry the patronymic *Michiels*: Jannetje (m. 1651 Jan Versteven), Gerret
(ondertrouw 1656), Jan (m. 1658, **buried Rotterdam 18 Jan 1660**), Maertien (m. 1663
Willem Maes), Flora (m. Heijndrick Balckeschoot), Francijntje (m. 1665 / 1668 / 1677),
Arijaentie. Third generation: **Lijntje Jans** (bapt. 1659; m. 1678 Lodewijk de Jonker;
children 1679, 1681; later Teunis Steck, 1694). Tail to Middelburg 1691–1727, Zierikzee
1696, Leiden 1724, and **Daniel Bundervoet in Batavia, 1707 and 1710 (VOC service)**.

Two internal confirmations make this a family rather than a name list: Jannetje names her
sons **Michiel** (1658) and **Gerrit** (1663) in the standard Dutch order, which is what
recovers *Geert* as the founding generation; and the witness at Lijntje's son's 1681
baptism is **"Francintie Machielse"**, the child's great-aunt.

**(b) 1818 onward, from Gent.** Livinus Cornelis (§3) and his descendants — Hermanus
Wilhelmus (1821–1860), Johanna Lowina Maria (1844–1925), then Delft, Rhoon,
Vlaardinger-Ambacht into the 1960s.

**They are separate.** WieWasWie and Open Archives together show **no Bundervoet anywhere
in the Netherlands between 1728 and 1813** — an 85-year gap. Group (a) also has almost no
surviving male line: Jan died in 1660 leaving a daughter, Gerret has no recorded children,
and every other child was a daughter who married out. The surname died in Rotterdam and
arrived again, independently, 90 years later.

## 6. Negatives and blocks, with their real extent

**A method warning, twice earned.** FamilySearch and Geneanet both label records with the
**collection's** date span (`1541-1950`, `1581-1910`, `1509-1920`). Parsed as a record
date, that manufactures 16th-century records that do not exist. I made this error once in
this pass and caught it; it is worth a guard in whatever ingests these files.

**`gnt` (Archief Gent) holds no 19th-century Gent civil registration.** Across all 361,158
held acts, BS Geboorte/Huwelijk/Overlijden all start effectively at **1900**; the pre-1901
material is the **Vondelingenregister**, 1790–1860.

**`abt` is a partial harvest, not an archive** — 5,585 acts, with **36 marriage acts for the
whole of the 1810s**. Any miss scoped to `abt` for a Gent marriage is scoped to almost
nothing.

**FamilySearch does not index Gent registers before ~1851.** Control: surname `De Coster`,
600 records from the same collection, 91 distinct register titles, **no Gent register
earlier than 1851**. This is why Gent looked empty and Evergem looked like the origin.

**AGATHA — `blocked`.** `/nl/search/persons/results/` now 404s; the surviving tabs are
`genealogie`, `analyses`, `ead`, and `genealogie` is a register-and-place browser with **no
person-name field**. Logged in fine as `shbunder`, so this is a changed URL scheme, not an
access failure. **Any earlier miss logged against an AGATHA person search should be
re-checked** — that route no longer exists.

**FamilySearch full-text — `blocked` by hCaptcha.** The SPA pages served a captcha
challenge for the whole session (the cookie-authed persona API kept working, which is why
the record search succeeded and full-text did not). Loading the page once by hand clears
it. This is the venue that would reach un-indexed Gent registers 1796–1850.

**WieWasWie — reachable in-browser only** (403 to unauthenticated requests). Swept: 128
records, all inside the two Dutch populations above.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN, but no longer closed.** No Gent patriarch is grafted and none should
be on this evidence. But four of the six now have marriage acts located, two of the
collateral couples are confirmed from the parish registers, the six look like one
intermarrying Gent network rather than six unrelated households, the Assenede branch is
documented, and the whole question now turns on **two named acts in Gent, 1787 and 22 May
1788**, either of which can be fetched.
